I'm a Ph.D. candidate in physics at Brown University, working with Prof. Gang
Xiao on magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ) sensors for ultrasensitive magnetic
field detection. My research asks what actually sets the smallest field an
MTJ can detect, and how to engineer devices that push that limit.

In vortex-state MTJs, I showed that a two-step orthogonal magnetic anneal
cuts the free layer's uniaxial anisotropy roughly eightfold, stabilizing a
single magnetic vortex in junctions as large as 40 μm. Those larger devices
reach sensitivities up to 2.80%/Oe — an eightfold gain over standard vortex
MTJs, whose diameters are limited to about 5 μm — while keeping a
non-hysteretic response
([*Phys. Rev. Applied*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/41by-5p3c)).

I've also revisited how MTJ sensitivity is measured in the first place. By
adapting the Jiles–Atherton model to junction conductance, I separated
reversible from irreversible magnetization processes down to the nanotesla
range, and showed that conventional minor-loop sensitivities can overstate
true small-field detectability by more than a factor of two
([*J. Phys. D*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6463/ae802f)).
